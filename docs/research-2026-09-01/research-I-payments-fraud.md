Direct Stripe checkout is roughly **2.5–3x cheaper per order** than Etsy at this AOV — the dominant lever isn't which Stripe add-ons to buy, it's already having left Etsy.

## 2. Must / Should / Nice checklist

### MUST

- **[M1] Finish the D1 deploy blocker before relying on anything else in this report.** `docs/STATE-LAYER.md` §Status states `wrangler.toml` still has a placeholder `database_id` and `wrangler deploy` fails until it's replaced by `wrangler d1 create yallternative-state`'s real output. Until that's live, `/api/stripe-webhook` and `/api/gift-card-balance` 503, and the webhook's exactly-once claim table is absent (it still works, relying on each sub-effect's own idempotency, but that's the fallback, not the design). This is an operational fact from the repo, not a Stripe recommendation — but it gates everything else here.
- **[M2] Get a real answer on the gift-card-coupon tax gap before Stripe Tax carries real registrations.** The code's own comment (`checkout.js` lines ~108–123) already identifies this: a gift-card redemption is sent to Stripe as a single-use `amount_off` coupon, so Stripe Tax rates the *discounted* subtotal. Tax law in most states treats a gift card as a form of tender, not a price reduction — the full pre-discount price should be taxed, with the card paying part of the resulting total. Every state taxes gift cards at redemption, not purchase ([Numeral 2026 guide](https://www.numeral.com/blog/sales-tax-on-discounts-coupons-gift-cards), [Brady Martz](https://www.bradymartz.com/the-fine-print-this-holiday-season-how-states-tax-gift-certificates/)), which is exactly the treatment this coupon mechanism breaks for the *portion* of an order a gift card covers. An all-gift-card order nets out correctly by coincidence (0% of $0 taxable = $0, and gift cards themselves are correctly coded `txcd_10502000` and untaxed at sale) — but a partially-covered order under-collects tax on the discounted slice. Stripe Checkout has no native "apply stored value as tender, tax the pre-discount price" primitive, so this can't be fixed in Checkout as configured; it needs either (a) computing tax before the coupon is applied and adding it as a separate non-discountable line, which Checkout doesn't support cleanly, or (b) accepting the gap and documenting it for whoever files SC sales tax returns. Either way, this needs a decision, not silence — the code comment shows the previous author understood the problem and shipped anyway.
- **[M3] Add `consent_collection[terms_of_service]` + linked Terms/Refund policy.** Checkout can require a Terms-of-Service checkbox and render your own linked terms via `custom_text.terms_of_service_acceptance` ([docs.stripe.com/payments/checkout/customization/policies](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/checkout/customization/policies)). None of the params in `handleCheckout` set this. Beyond the legal-clarity value, an accepted TOS + a stated refund policy is standard "product as described" dispute evidence — currently absent, which weakens future dispute defense for zero implementation cost (one Dashboard-configurable param).
- **[M4] Add Radar rules aimed specifically at gift-card abuse, not just generic card fraud.** Gift cards are reported ~4x riskier than physical goods because a stolen card can be laundered into resellable stored value in one transaction with no shipping delay to catch it ([Riskified](https://www.riskified.com/learning/ecommerce-fraud/gift-card-fraud/), [Chargeback Gurus](https://www.chargebackgurus.com/blog/gift-card-fraud)). Recommended rules (configurable free in Radar, [docs.stripe.com/radar/rules](https://docs.stripe.com/radar/rules)): review/block a gift-card-only order where billing country ≠ card-issuing country; review first-seen card + gift-card purchase over ~$100; velocity-limit gift-card purchases per card/email/IP per day. This is the fraud vector this integration is structurally most exposed to (email-delivered stored value, no physical fulfillment delay as a natural brake) and none of the reviewed code applies gift-card-specific risk logic — the only defenses in place are the ledger's reserve/commit correctness (real, but orthogonal to whether the *purchase itself* was fraudulent) and generic Radar defaults.

### SHOULD

- **[S1] Turn on Checkout's built-in abandoned-cart recovery.** It's a Dashboard/param flip, not a build: set `after_expiration[recovery][enabled]=true` plus `consent_collection[promotions]=auto`, then listen for `checkout.session.expired`'s `after_expiration.recovery.url` (valid 30 days) and email it ([docs.stripe.com/payments/checkout/abandoned-carts](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/checkout/abandoned-carts.md?payment-ui=stripe-hosted)). The Worker already has a live Resend integration (used for gift-card email) that could send this with one more template. Mostly-mobile traffic with a redirect-to-hosted-page flow is exactly the profile that abandons at the handoff; this is free revenue recovery currently left off. Note: recovering carts requires *promotional* email consent — a customer who declines still gets no recovery email, by design (CAN-SPAM/consent rules), so this doesn't fully replace cart-abandonment analytics.
- **[S2] Enable Chargeback Protection once eligible — likely not yet, revisit at 6 months clean history.** It reimburses card-not-present "unauthorized use" disputes on Checkout orders with no evidence burden and waives the $15 dispute fee, for 0.4% on protected transactions only ([chargeback.io](https://www.chargeback.io/blog/what-is-stripe-chargeback-protection)); typical eligibility needs ~6 months of processing history, Checkout-exclusive routing (this shop qualifies structurally — it's 100% hosted Checkout), and no manually-overridden charges. It does **not** cover "product not received," friendly fraud, or non-fraud reason codes — so it complements but doesn't replace S3's evidence discipline, and doesn't cover the fraud-adjacent gift-card-resale scenario in M4 unless the specific dispute reason is "unauthorized."
- **[S3] Formalize shipping-evidence discipline for "product not received" disputes** — the other common dispute type Chargeback Protection doesn't cover. Evidence submitted *after delivery confirmation* wins ~27 points more often than evidence with no delivery confirmation; Stripe's own Smart Disputes can auto-pull tracking history from 12+ carriers if given carrier + tracking number ([Stripe blog analysis](https://stripe.com/blog/analyzing-the-evidence-that-helps-businesses-win-product-not-received-disputes)). This is a process recommendation (does fulfillment log tracking numbers anywhere Stripe can see them?), not a code change — nothing in the reviewed Worker code attaches tracking to the PaymentIntent/order.
- **[S4] Turn on Adaptive Pricing for Checkout.** Free, Dashboard opt-in toggle, no code change ([docs.stripe.com/payments/checkout/localize-prices/adaptive-pricing](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/checkout/localize-prices/adaptive-pricing)); presents 150+ local currencies while your integration currency, PaymentIntent, and this Worker's own USD math stay untouched (`presentment_details` carries the local-currency figure separately) — so it's safe to enable without touching `checkout.js`'s cents math at all. Low priority for a Landrum-market/SC-tax-focused shop with presumably mostly-US buyers, but zero-cost to turn on.
- **[S5] Periodically audit which payment methods are actually live in the Dashboard.** `checkout.js` deliberately omits `payment_method_types` so Stripe can offer whatever the Dashboard has enabled (a good call, documented in the file's own comments — pinning the list previously fought the Dashboard settings). The flip side: Stripe can also *auto-enable new methods* for the account without a code review gate. Recommend a quarterly look at Settings → Payment methods rather than assuming the list hasn't drifted.
- **[S6] Complete the PCI SAQ-A self-attestation annually.** Because 100% of card entry happens on `checkout.stripe.com` (hosted redirect) and card data never touches this Worker, the integration qualifies for the lightest PCI tier, SAQ A ([docs.stripe.com/security/guide](https://docs.stripe.com/security/guide), [stripe.com/guides/pci-compliance](https://stripe.com/guides/pci-compliance)). Stripe being PCI Level 1 certified does not remove the merchant's own annual attestation obligation — that's on the shop owner via Dashboard → Settings → Compliance documents, easy to forget for a one-person operation.
- **[S7] Cash App Pay: enable if not already — no separate fee, no code change needed.** US-only, B2C, USD-only account requirements are already met by this business; supported natively in Checkout ([docs.stripe.com/payments/cash-app-pay](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/cash-app-pay)). Given "mostly mobile" traffic and a body-care/handmade-goods demographic that skews toward exactly Cash App's user base, this is close to free upside; the omission of `payment_method_types` in the Worker means this is purely a Dashboard toggle.

### NICE

- **[N1] BNPL (Affirm/Klarna/Afterpay) — probably skip at this AOV, revisit if data says otherwise.** Stripe and Klarna both cite large conversion/AOV lifts (up to 14% revenue lift on BNPL-eligible sessions per Stripe; 30%/41% per Klarna's own research — [stripe.com/guides/buy-now-pay-later](https://stripe.com/guides/buy-now-pay-later)), but that data comes overwhelmingly from higher-ticket verticals (furniture, electronics, $75+ apparel) where splitting payments meaningfully changes a buyer's yes/no decision. At $20–60 AOV, a 4-payment split is $5–15/installment — the psychological unlock BNPL sells is much weaker, and it adds a second dispute/refund process to reconcile for a single operator. Worth a real test only if cart-abandonment data shows concentration at the top of the order-value range.
- **[N2] Phone number collection — skip unless it's actually used.** `phone_number_collection` is a one-line param but adds checkout friction for no benefit unless someone downstream (market pickup coordination, SMS shipping updates) reads the number. Nothing in the reviewed code currently consumes a phone field.
- **[N3] Stripe Dashboard mobile app** for on-the-go order/dispute monitoring — fits a market-pop-up business model where the owner isn't at a desk.
- **[N4] Instant Payouts — skip.** Standard 2-business-day rolling payout is free and already fast; the ~1%+$0.50 fee only makes sense against a real cash-flow emergency, not routine use for a $20–60-AOV shop.
- **[N5] Branded receipt customization** (logo, support email, business name) in Dashboard branding settings — cosmetic trust signal, no code involved.

## 4. Notes on things explicitly asked about but not separately broken out above

- **Saved payment methods / `customer_creation`:** Set to `"always"` only when Tax is on and the order isn't pinned to a market Customer (mutually exclusive with reusing the pinned `customer`), matching Stripe's requirement that a Customer exist to hold a collected address. For an average $20–60 mostly-one-off body-care purchase, there's little incentive to push saved-card enrollment harder than this — Link (enabled automatically since `payment_method_types` is unset) already gives repeat buyers 1-click checkout without the shop needing its own saved-card UI.
- **Shipping via `shipping_options` vs. a hardcoded Worker line:** current design computes the free-shipping/flat-$10 decision in the Worker (server-validated against the CMS-editable threshold) and passes the *result* as a single `shipping_options[0]` entry to Checkout — correct: Stripe's `shipping_options` array is meant for offering real customer choice among named services, which this shop doesn't need (flat rate or free), so a single computed option is the right-sized use of the parameter rather than under- or over-using it.
- **SCA/3DS in the US:** `request_three_d_secure: "automatic"` is Stripe's own recommended default for US issuers, where 3DS/SCA isn't mandated the way it is under EU PSD2 — Stripe steps up only when the issuer or Radar's own risk signal calls for it. No change recommended.
- **Stripe Tax registration vs. what Stripe Tax does NOT do:** Stripe Tax calculates and can *optionally* auto-file in supported states (South Carolina is one, via TaxJar-powered Dashboard filing per [docs.stripe.com/tax/supported-countries/united-states/south-carolina](https://docs.stripe.com/tax/supported-countries/united-states/south-carolina)), and can register on the merchant's behalf via a paid assist — but it does not decide *whether* you're obligated to register beyond flagging threshold crossings (SC's remote-seller threshold is $100,000 gross sales/yr, well above this shop's stated volume, so **physical presence in SC is what creates the obligation here, not the economic-nexus threshold** — worth having an accountant confirm registration is filed on the right basis, not assumed via the threshold monitor).
- **SC shipping taxability:** shipping is taxable in SC when the goods shipped are taxable and delivery terms make it part of the sale (common carrier, unspecified/F.O.B.-destination terms) — which is exactly why the Worker tags the shipping line with `txcd_92010001` and `exclusive` tax behavior rather than hardcoding an assumption; that's the correct approach given SC's shipment-terms-dependent rule ([Byte Orca SC shipping guide](https://www.byteorca.com/en/blog/south-carolina-shipping-sales-tax); [dor.sc.gov RR19-9](https://dor.sc.gov/sites/dor/files/policies/RR19-9.pdf)).
